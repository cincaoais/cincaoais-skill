# Section 6: Types of Liquidity — Retail, Smart Money Trap, Session, Daily Candle
> **Source**: *E-book Smart Money SMC* (anonymous, 33 pp., translated), PDF pp. 16–21

*Terminology note: "withdrawal/removal/extraction of liquidity" = liquidity sweep; "SMT" here = **Smart Money Trap** (not the SMT-divergence of other SMC schools); "countersell" = counter-trend short.*

## Core Idea
Every obvious level retail traders use — trendlines, S/R, equal highs/lows, session highs/lows, the previous candle's high/low — is **stop-loss liquidity**. Institutions trade *against* those patterns. Wait for the liquidity to be taken, then trade the reaction from a POI with LTF confirmation. The **first pullback/OB after a BOS is always a trap**.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Retail liquidity (patterns, S/R, EQH/EQL, trendlines)**:
  - When to use: whenever a textbook retail setup appears near your POI.
  - How: (1) mark the retail level (resistance/EQH, support/EQL, trendline); (2) don't enter at it even if an OB sits there — wait until all its liquidity is withdrawn; (3) on a trendline break, **fade the break**: break up → look to sell, break down → look to buy; (4) after the break, find the IDM and the first OB beyond it; (5) enter after LTF confirmation (e.g. one-candle formation). SnR/EQH sequence (p. 16): after the sweep of the equal highs, wait for the CHoCH below, then the IDM, then sell at the supply with a one-candle entry.
- **Smart Money Trap (SMT) / IDM**: The OB is not the edge — it's only extra confirmation. The first pullback after a BOS, or the first OB, is always a trap and acts as inducement.
  - How: (1) after BOS, label the first pullback/OB as SMT; (2) don't trade it; (3) wait for price to sweep the IDM (or the SMT OB) ; (4) enter at the next OB (ideally the extreme OB) on a one-candle entry. If price can't close with a body beyond the previous high, carry the high and the IDM forward.
- **Session liquidity (time + price)**: Every session high/low is liquidity; taking it tends to produce a reversal. At least one session per day is manipulative.
  - Rules: if **Asia + London continue each other's trend → New York reverses** (manipulates London). If **London swept Asia's high or low → New York continues in London's direction**.
  - How: (1) box Asia, London, NY ranges; (2) wait for a session high/low sweep; (3) when price closes back inside the session range, enter via one-candle entry or LTF CHoCH; (4) if a candle **closes with a body beyond** the session high/low, it is not a sweep-sell/buy — trade it only after an LTF CHoCH confirms ("when price closes above the body, it is not set to sell, but you can sell here after confirming CHoCH", p. 20).
- **Daily candle liquidity**: Traders sell breaks of the previous candle's low and buy breaks of its high; those are liquidity.
  - How: (1) define daily momentum; (2) bearish momentum + previous daily **high** swept → high-probability sell; (3) bullish momentum + previous **low** swept → high-probability buy; (4) counter-momentum sweeps allow only short-term "counter" trades; (5) after the sweep, drop to **M15** and wait for the nearest structure break to confirm the day's direction; (6) momentum can shift: once it turns bullish after a bearish run, treat the next previous-candle low sweep as a high-probability buy (candles A, K, p. 21); (7) when both the previous high and low are in play, either side is tradable, but favour the **short-term trend** direction ("the probability of selling is high, as the short-term trend is bearish", p. 21).

## Key Concepts
- **Retail demand/supply zone**: a zone retail keeps buying/selling that fails against SMC momentum.
- **EQH / EQL**: equal highs/lows = resting stops.
- **Trendline trap**: breakout that induces retail before reversing.
- **SMT (Smart Money Trap)**: first pullback/OB after BOS; always inducement.
- **Session high/low**: Asia, London, New York extremes; liquidity pools.
- **Manipulative session**: the session that sweeps another session's extreme (interpretation — not stated in SE; the book only says at least one session per day is manipulative).
- **Countersell / counter-buy**: short-term trade against daily momentum after a candle sweep.

## Mental Models
- Think "**where are the stops?**" before "where is support?".
- Use the **session chain**: Asia sets the range → London either continues or sweeps → NY finishes or reverses.
- Treat the first OB after BOS as **bait**, the extreme OB as **the meal**.

## Anti-patterns
- **Selling resistance/EQH on first touch**: wait for the sweep, even with an OB there.
- **Buying the trendline breakout**: that's the retail side of the trap.
- **Trading the SMT OB**: "Don't buy here because it's SMT — buy below IDM."
- **Treating a body close beyond a session high/low as a sweep**: it's a break; wait for an LTF CHoCH.
- **Selling a second session sweep** when Asia's liquidity was already manipulated earlier: one manipulation per day is the higher-probability one.
- **Counter-trading daily momentum** as if it were a full reversal.

## Worked Example
p. 16 top (retail demand zone): in bearish SMC momentum the retail demand zone is repeatedly defended and then broken; pullbacks into supply become successive **SELL ENTRY** levels stepping down (the later two are marked beside an IDM x).

p. 17 trendline trap (bearish): price rallies, takes the **IDM** ("first confirmation for smart money"), then breaks the recent low as a **CHoCH** ("second confirmation"). Price drifts up along a descending trendline; retail sees a "strong bullish breakout" through it. That breakout runs into the supply OB above the IDM and trendline — "collect all retail money based on trendline and breakout" — and the sell from that OB drives price far lower. p. 17 lower (bullish): retail buys the demand zone; price sweeps below it into the POI, prints a CHoCH, then each downside break of the rising trendline traps sellers; buy at the OB after the trendline trap.

p. 20 session example: Asia and London both push down; NY sweeps the **Asian low** and then the **London low**; the first reversal zone after the inducement is taken at NY open; "if the zone fails, buy after the main-low / London-liquidity sweep" — price then rallies hard.

One p. 21 caption stays unresolved; it reads, as printed: "Short term purchase. After the previous maximum is taken. Because the main movement is bullish." — a short-term buy in line with the bullish main movement after the previous high is taken, which doesn't map cleanly onto the daily table above.

## Key Takeaways
1. Retail levels are liquidity; wait for their sweep.
2. Fade trendline breakouts using IDM + first OB beyond + LTF confirmation.
3. The first pullback/OB after BOS is a trap (SMT).
4. One session per day manipulates; use the Asia/London → NY rules.
5. Previous-day high/low sweeps with momentum are high-probability; confirm on M15.
