# Chapter 3: Liquidity and Premium/Discount Zones
> **Source**: *Smart Money Concepts: Unlocking the Path to High Probability Trading* — Frank Miller (2024), Ch 3, PDF pp. 24–41

## Core Idea
The market must trigger stop losses (liquidity) to fund the next move, so the highest-probability zones are demand/supply areas that sit *beyond* an obvious liquidity pool — and longs belong in the **discount** half (below 50%) of the current impulse, shorts in the **premium** half.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Liquidity as fuel**: price sweeps highs/lows to trigger stops and pending orders; that captured money pays the winners.
  - When to use: before any entry, ask "has the liquidity in front of my zone been taken yet?"
  - How: locate retail stop clusters, expect price to sweep them, and only trade the zone *behind* the swept pool.
- **Four Liquidity Models** (Miller's "keep these four firmly in mind"):
  1. **Structural Low/High** — stops under a pullback low / above a pullback high. Look left: an unfilled gap (imbalance) below a demand zone acts as a magnet, so a drop into it is likely a sweep, not a reversal.
  2. **Equal Highs/Lows** — double/triple tops and bottoms. Retail enters on the pattern and parks stops just beyond it → large pool. Typical formation (bullish): after a BOS the retrace stops short of the demand zone left by the BOS wave, the next push fails to break the major high, and the next dip holds the same low → double bottom, with an unmitigated extreme demand zone still sitting beneath the pool (mirror for EQH).
  3. **Trendline Liquidity** — a slow, tight trendline of minor highs/lows after an impulsive move with inefficiency; retail stops sit just beyond the trendline. A trendline needs at least two lows or two highs.
  4. **Sideways (Range) Liquidity** — a range is market indecision, a pause phase before or after a big move. Retail buys the lower edge (stops below) and sells the upper edge (stops above), so stops sit beyond both boundaries.
- **Zone-validity rule**: only treat a demand/supply zone as tradeable if a liquidity sweep pattern has occurred before price reaches it. A zone reached *without* a sweep becomes the liquidity pool for a deeper zone.
- **Premium & Discount (Fibonacci 50%)**:
  - When to use: to time entries within a correction and choose between zones. It solves the entry dilemma: enter too early and a deeper pullback stops you out even though the analysis was right; wait too long and price leaves without you.
  - How:
    1. Draw Fibonacci from the start (A) to the end (B) of the latest impulsive wave.
    2. Use only the 50% level = **equilibrium**.
    3. Uptrend: buy only in **discount** (below 50%). Downtrend: sell only in **premium** (above 50%).
    4. Stop beyond A; first target B.

## Key Concepts
- **Liquidity**: resting stop losses and pending orders around obvious highs/lows.
- **Liquidity pool**: cluster of such orders (equal lows, trendline, range edge).
- **Liquidity sweep**: price pushing through a pool before moving in the intended direction. A wick *or* a close through the level both count as a sweep; the wick-vs-close test only matters when confirming a BOS/CHoCH (Ch 2).
- **Equal Highs (EQH) / Equal Lows (EQL)**: internal highs/lows at the same level; double or triple formations.
- **Inefficiency / gap**: imbalance left by a fast move; draws price back.
- **Fibonacci retracement levels**: drawn from impulse start to end, each level can act as support (resistance in a downtrend); the lower the level in an uptrend, the deeper the retracement. Miller only needs the 50% line.
- **Equilibrium**: the 50% level of the impulse range.
- **Premium zone**: above 50% — expensive; sell side.
- **Discount zone**: below 50% — cheap; buy side.

## Mental Models
- Think "no one stopped out, no money to pay winners" — sweeps are the market's funding mechanism.
- Read impulse/correction as a sentiment fractal: optimism drives buying and the impulse; as price looks overvalued, profit-taking and early shorts create the pullback (downtrend mirror: pessimism drives selling, "oversold" buy-backs create the retracement).
- Use retail pattern trading (double bottoms, trendlines, range edges) as a map of where stops sit.
- Think of an unswept zone as bait: it will likely be run through to reach the deeper zone.
- Prefer a missed trade to a losing one — waiting for discount costs some trades but raises win rate.
- Use the 50% line as a fast filter: wrong half = no trade.

## Anti-patterns
- **Stops just below an obvious low / above an obvious high**: this is exactly the liquidity that gets swept.
- **Buying the double bottom / selling the double top**: you become the pool.
- **Trading the first zone price reaches when no liquidity was swept**: it is likely to be used as liquidity.
- **Buying in premium during an uptrend**: poor R:R (first target B is close), wider stop, early-buyer trap.

## Reference Tables

| Liquidity model | Where stops sit | What to wait for | Trade from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Structural low/high | Beyond pullback extreme | Sweep of that extreme (+ gap fill) | Demand/supply just beyond |
| Equal lows / highs | Just beyond double/triple level | Sweep of the equal level | Extreme demand/supply beyond pool |
| Trendline | Just beyond trendline | Break through trendline + inefficiency fill | Zone at the extreme of the move |
| Sideways range | Beyond both boundaries | Sweep of the range edge | Demand (uptrend) / supply (downtrend) beyond edge |

| Premium/Discount reason (uptrend) | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Reason No.1: Safer Entry | Market takes out early buyers first |
| Reason No.2: Higher Risk to Reward Ratio | First target is B; entering high shrinks reward; always want > 1:1 |
| Reason No.3: Safer Stop | Stop just below swing low A, which bulls defend. A break below A means buyers have lost control (reversal risk) — unless it is a fakeout that grabs liquidity below A and then takes off |

## Worked Example
Miller's live trade: at the end of a downtrend he found a demand zone whose last candle had swept the previous bearish candle's liquidity. He then checked for a pool *above* the zone: after two major lows formed, price consolidated in small directionless candles along a trendline, so the whole area beneath those two lows became the liquidity zone. One strong bearish candle swept all of it and tapped the demand zone; the buy limit triggered and price rallied through previous major highs. Lesson: zone quality = sweep inside the zone + untouched pool in front of it.

Equal-lows illustration: in an uptrend with two candidate demand areas, he rejects the upper one (no liquidity swept before reaching it) and treats it plus the equal lows as the pool. He waits for price to sweep below the equal lows and enter the lower demand zone before buying.

## Key Takeaways
1. Mark liquidity pools first; zones are only as good as the liquidity swept before them.
2. Four models: structural highs/lows, equal highs/lows, trendlines, ranges.
3. An unswept zone will likely become liquidity for a deeper zone.
4. Look left for gaps/imbalances that pull price into a sweep.
5. Buy in discount, sell in premium, measured with the 50% of the latest impulse.
6. Stop beyond the impulse origin (A); first target the impulse end (B).
